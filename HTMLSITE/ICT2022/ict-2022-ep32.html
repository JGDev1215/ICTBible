Consolidation Day, Outside Day & the 3–4 PM Window

How to read a choppy consolidation day: the outside day definition, why price gravitates to the middle of range, and why the 3–4 PM window is when the algorithm delivers on days like this. E-mini S&P.

Definition

Outside Day

Outside Day Defined

"An outside day is a day that's higher than the previous day and lower than the previous day's low. So here's previous day's high — it went higher. His previous day's low — it went lower. So all of this range is outside of the previous day's range. And it closed down."

1:37

"When this happens there's typically this type of price action formation — but it's really included with the odds in your favor if you're trading down to an old low but fall short of it. This is usually where that double bottom idea comes in and it feels safe. To — if you're trading like me — look for a run through that old low. But if they stop it short it tends to be a choppy day, especially if we have an outside date with down close."

Outside Day + Down Close → Choppy Day

When there is an outside day with a down close and price falls short of running through the old low: "it tends to be a choppy day." On this session, price came within one handle of the 38.55 old low (38.56) before reversing higher.

Concept

Consolidation Day

Consolidation Day Defined

"The algorithm on days like this — this is called a consolidation day. And what happens is this: the market starts trading, creates an initial range and then stays in that range until the afternoon at three o'clock."

10:58

"All of this in here is really hard choppy price action — it's very very difficult to trade in those types of ranges because they can come back on you, stop you out, and just go nowhere for a long period of time."

3:56

"This imbalance here — the fair value gap — it should have offered a little bit more movement lower. Why wouldn't it go down and take out this short-term low? It went below this short-term low here and then started rallying, working towards the relative equal highs over here."

5:55

"After that it just goes back and forth chopping — short-term lows are taken, high is taken here, we trade back and forth. All of this is really ugly type of price action."

Time Concept

The 3–4 PM Window on Consolidation Days

3–4 PM Window Defined

"Wait until three o'clock because many times at three o'clock to four — the algorithm will do something where it goes outside the bounds of the daily range. It trades up into it and then rams it down."

11:28

"The market starts trading, creates an initial range and then stays in that range until the afternoon at three o'clock. It used to be the bond close that started this whole thing but now it's just three o'clock — at four o'clock when equities close, the closing bell — that sets the tone for on-close orders."

7:57

"I gave a very specific window between three o'clock and four o'clock in the afternoon."

17:44

"If you're caught up in it — you know what you're doing or you feel like it's just being chopped up — wait until three o'clock because many times at three o'clock to four the algorithm will do something where it goes outside the bounds of the daily range like it did here. It trades up into it and then rams it down."

Why On-Close Orders Matter

"At four o'clock when equities close, the closing bell — that sets the tone for on-close orders. So what that means is what was going on prior to three o'clock — the market was rallying up. What did it go up for? The buy-side liquidity."

Concept

Buy-Side Liquidity Run → Short at 3 PM

7:21

"We ran above the short-term high, so we have relative equal highs here and relative equal highs here — so there's buy-side resting here. I outlined how the market would use the running up to take out the buy stops to squeeze anybody that was short and trail their stop-loss down, and also to induce people into thinking it's going to go higher — so that way it builds up liquidity below these lows. What kind of liquidity? Sell-side liquidity, because they're going to protect their long positions."

16:41

"All the buy orders that were resting above here are here in the form of protecting short positions acting as their stop-loss order. When the market trades above that — those buy stops that are protective in nature — they become a rushing liquidity wave of willing buyers at the market."

16:56

"Willing buyers at the market at three o'clock on a day like this where it's consolidated — it's going to want to go back to the middle of the range."

Consolidation day structure — 3–4 PM window, buy-side run above rel. equal highs → FVG → middle of range target (1:17–17:58)
  • 01 Before 3pm: choppy range, 50/50 probability. "It really statistically moves to 50/50 when we start trading back into the middle of the range."
  • 02 At 3pm: market rallies above relative equal highs, runs buy stops (protective stops of short sellers become market orders): "a rushing liquidity wave of willing buyers at the market."
  • 03 The run above creates a swing low and displacement leg with a fair value gap: "is this a swing low? Yes. Is this a fair value gap? Yes. Does it trade back up into that range? Yes."
  • 04 Target: "the middle of the range." Short entry, partial at midpoint. ICT sold short at 3940.75, took 4 partials off at 3908.75 (middle of range).
Concept

Middle of the Daily Range as Target

7:05

"If you look at what the high was and the low — right in here — if that's the middle of the range, we ran above the short-term high so we have relative equal highs here. I outlined how the market would use the running up to take out the buy stops to squeeze anybody that was short — and then pull back to the middle of the range."

17:09

"It's going to want to go back to the middle of the range. So when you look at price action days like this — and if you're caught up in it, you know what you're doing or you feel like it's just being chopped up — wait until three o'clock."

How to Measure the Middle of Range

Use the high and low of the initial session range (approximately 9:30 AM onward): "So the low of the day there and the high of the day there — split that in the middle, there's your midpoint — and it's going to want to gravitate back to that."

Definition

Dealing Range

Dealing Range Defined

"We have to take every single trade idea based on its current dealing ranges. And what's a dealing range? Well, a dealing range would be like from this high down to that low — and we measure those."

5:35

"Yes, it trades up into that imbalance and trades down to a short-term low — that's a tradable opportunity there."
