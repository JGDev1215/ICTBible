Core Concept

Order Block — Three Sensitive Areas

0:38

"When we're looking at a candle that we're using for like an order block — the low, the open, and the middle of the range — those are the three sensitive areas that you're watching for."

Area 1
Low

The candle low — first reference level of the order block.

Area 2
Open

The candle open — second reference level inside the order block.

Area 3
Middle of Range

"This is what I call the mean threshold." — the 50% level of the candle's range.

Three sensitive areas of a bearish order block candle — Low, Open, Mean Threshold (0:38–1:08)
Definition

Mean Threshold

Mean Threshold Defined

"If the market trades up into the middle point of that candle — this is what I call the mean threshold."

0:58

"Mean threshold was taken today — and that to me bodes well for a continuation to take out this short-term high."

How to Measure It

"If you take your fib and you run it across this candle to [the] 50 level." The mean threshold is the 50% level of the order block candle's full range (high to low).

What It Signals

ICT describes price digging up through the order block: "We watch to see if it gets to the mean threshold — which is half of that [order block candle] on the daily chart." Reaching the mean threshold indicates continuation is likely through to the candle's open and potentially beyond.

Concept — Refined

SMT Divergence — When It Typically Occurs

7:29

"At certain times — what times? 8:30, 9:30, at news events being released — they'll create this divergence. And if you have a bias it's helpful. But if you don't know where it's going, you're going to many times encounter what would look like SMT divergence and then it disappears as they start moving in concert with one another. So you have to be careful with that — that's why I always teach: know what it's reaching for."

Critical Caveat

"If you don't know where it's going, you're going to many times encounter what would look like SMT divergence and then it disappears as they start moving in concert with one another." Bias must come first.

7:04

"There's a cracking correlation between the two — usually they move in tandem. But at certain times they'll create this divergence."

8:23

"If you look at the relationship of that fair value gap — dropping back down in, higher low than that of [8:30] versus the lower low made in NASDAQ — this is a stop out, this is accumulation of longs, this is going to be the telltale sign that it's going to go higher."

Timing Rule

Early Run Before 7 AM → Expect SMT at 9:30

The Rule

"If I get an early run like this, my first thought is I need to go to SMT later on at 9:30 because it's probably going to require some kind of a cracking correlation to set up the next leg if it's going to have one."

14:56

"If I can see this setup early in the day — before the seven o'clock in the morning — to me it gives me insight, it gives me an expectation around what the 9:30 opening is going to be like."

13:43

"Because it gave a nice run here earlier before seven o'clock in the morning — that means we're probably going to have a deep retracement and look for an ideal scenario like we outlined with the SMT divergence."

  • 01 A meaningful run occurs before 7 AM (or near 7 AM).
  • 02 Expectation: a deep retracement follows that early run.
  • 03 Look for SMT divergence at 9:30 to confirm the next leg: "It's probably going to require some kind of a cracking correlation to set up the next leg."
Concept

High Resistance Liquidity Run

12:05

"We have all this range in here that it's not likely to go straight all the way through all this stuff — that's a high resistance liquidity run. Not that it can't happen but it's likely to not form or pan out because we had so many supporting ideas in here."

High Resistance Liquidity Run

A run toward liquidity that must pass through a large amount of overhead price range, imbalances, or supporting candles. ICT contrasts this with the underlying bullish order flow: "It's just finding underlying order flow that is bullish — so it's likely to go up, just to keep going higher, not go up to go down. Because to go up to go down we have all of these ranges that we would have to pierce and break."

Calendar Rule

Holiday Weekend / Light Volume Session Rules

15:53

"If you're going to be trading — study in the morning session, leave the afternoon to the gamblers. Volume just probably won't be on your side in the afternoon. Everybody's going to be going away for a long weekend."

  • 01 Friday before a holiday weekend: trade the morning session only. "Study in the morning session, leave the afternoon to the gamblers."
  • 02 Holiday Monday: do not trade. "Don't trade on Monday — take the day off."
  • 03 Reason: "Volume just probably won't be on your side in the afternoon — everybody's going to be going away for a long weekend."
