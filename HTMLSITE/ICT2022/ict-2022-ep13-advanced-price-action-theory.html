Down Closed Candle / Up Closed Candle Rules

Bullish Market Move

"If the move has been bullish — down closed candles should not be violated. Down closed candles should not be violated. They're going to act as support."

13:37
Bearish Market Move

"Bearish market moves — up close candles should not be breached and broken through. Never even come back."

13:51

"If price is going higher — down closed candles should support price."

22:19

Application in a Bullish Leg

"All the down closed candles — does it go below this down close candle when it starts to retrace? No. We expect this to be areas where they're going to accumulate more long positions. We retrace going down into an imbalance and the market rallies above the down close candle. Does it support price? Yes."

15:28

"I'm not worrying about the market as long as it doesn't take out these two down close candles."

22:56

"Trades down — gets real close to that — but does it eat up and go through that range of these two down close candles? No. It just retraces down into what — an imbalance. Why? So smart money can buy again."

23:02

When Down Closed Candles Are Broken

"If it comes down and breaks the low of these down closed candles — well there you go. You probably did the right thing by getting stopped down because it might be failing and going lower."

26:22

The Sequence Repeated

"Order block — trades through it here, get long. Fair value gap — order block — retracement — get long. Order block — imbalance with the fair value gap — trades back down — doesn't even come back down into the order block. This is classic — it generally doesn't like to go all the way back in rebalance when it's that close to the profit objective. So the algorithm only has a small little retracement inside the fair value gap so you would be a buyer."

Intermediate Term Low

"We do have a low that has a higher low to the left and a higher low to the right. So that makes this what — intermediate term low."

16:10

"When it fills in that gap — once that happens — this becomes an intermediate term low. This low should not be taken out once it starts rallying. It should not come back down here."

22:05

"Remember — intermediate term low is down here. This should never be overtaken until the objective's reached."

24:28

"So all this price action leg here is — I trust that it's going to keep going higher as long as the down closed candles keep price above it. Above what? Above the down close candles that's going to be supporting each new leg higher. The market should not retrace back down below those."

24:39

Intermediate Term Low: higher low left, the low itself, higher low right — should not be overtaken until objective reached

04

Trailing Stop Logic Based on Down Closed Candles

"For trailing stop losses — you do not want to take your stop loss if say you brought it down here like I did and your stop was below the midline of this candle — that's the mean threshold of the bullish order block — so I don't think it's going to go down below that once I'm entering."

25:01

"Then it starts to rally, goes above this candle's high — my stop-loss must remain below this candle's low because it can dig into that candle because it's going to act as an order block."

25:15

"The market rallies above, takes out these two down closed candles — now the stop can be raised from whatever would be below this down close candle. Now it can be raised below this down closed candle because the idea is these down closed candles are one order block that should not be violated if it's bullish."

26:03

Premature Stop Loss

"Now if I put my stop loss inside this area here I'm stopped out prematurely. Chances are you probably wouldn't have the wherewithal to get back in. You'd think well I just lost out one potential profit — I made a little gain but I'm afraid to get back in because it took me out. That's infancy. That's because you don't know what you're doing."

25:30

Pyramiding — Position Sizing Method

"I'm pyramiding. I'm building the biggest position initially and then every time I buy in again I'm building it with a smaller position than I had prior to when I'm entering now."

32:39
Pyramid Position Sizing — Bullish Example
Entry 1"I'm buying three here."
Entry 2"Then I'm buying two here."
Entry 3"Then I'm buying one here."
Then"I'm letting it run to my profit objective."

"I consider this all one trade. I don't consider this three separate trades. It's one trade — scaled in largest, middle, last portion."

33:53

"If I say I want to be a buyer of one here, two here, three here — that's an inverted pyramid. It's not stable. Imagine a pyramid upside down and it's balancing on its point — it's not solid, it's wobbly."

33:05

"I'm building the biggest base at the bottom of the pyramid or position size in its initial position entry."

33:19

"I have all the equity behind that entry with three supporting the two I'm buying here. So even if it starts to retrace too deep on me I have the ability to weather a little bit more."

32:53

Stable pyramid: largest first, then smaller — vs inverted pyramid: growing with each entry (unstable)

06

Demo Account — When and Why

"How do I manage that desire to get back in and do some more? I go right to a demo account. I work inside of a paper account. It provides me context. It keeps me glued — obviously — to what the market's doing so I'm staying plugged in."

7:49

"I'm not taking on monetary risk when I don't believe it's wise for me to do so. Because if I'm wrong — let's say I'm wrong — and I traded today with live funds and I lose what I gained in February, that would eat at me all through the month of March. So it's toxic thinking and I learned that the hard way many years ago."

8:17

"They'll fail on the demo account just as easily as they would be failing in the live account."

Live Trading Readiness

"If you feel this tug of war, this butterfly feeling in your stomach where you just can't relax and you're engaging in the marketplace — you're not ready. That's the bottom line — you're not ready. You have to desensitize yourself with the results not being a factor. If you lose it doesn't make a difference to you — you're indifferent to it."

6:01

"A student that has a solid price action model, a trade plan that's well documented — they know what they're going to do, why they're doing it, what they're waiting for, what will cause them to push the button to get in, where their stop loss is going to be, how much they're going to risk, what they're going to aim for, where their partials are going to be if they're going to take partials at all. You have to know those things in advance. You can't determine them once you enter the marketplace."

6:33
Conditions Before Considering Live Trading
Documented plan"A trading plan that is well documented — you know what it is you're doing, when you're not going to do something."
In advance"You have to know those things in advance — you can't determine them once you enter the marketplace."
Demo first"You've been consistent with a demo account for six months."
Only then"Then and only then — would I not necessarily that you need to do this — but I would then only at that time consider maybe going into live fund trading."

"Experience has taught me that I got into live trading way too fast — way, way too fast — in 1992. That was the worst thing I could have done for my development was rush to get into real money trading and I had no idea what I was doing."

Goal Setting & Frequency

2:56

"I set very low hanging objectives in front of me."

38:17

"If you don't aim for a target you're going to hit nothing 100 of the time. You have to have a goal."

One Trade a Week

"What happens if you do that once a week — and that's your trade — and you stop? And once you hit it you end your trading. That's it. And then you go to a demo account. You have any idea the leaps and bounds that you can have in your equity increasing and the peace of mind knowing that you don't have to over trade."

35:27

"I'm not trying to create monsters that trade every single day. Because I say these likely form every single day but they absolutely form every single week. That is not an invitation for you to go out and say ICT said trade every day. No it's not what I said. I'm saying if you miss a trade you're likely to find one tomorrow."

35:57

Six Months of Practice

"I believe in six months of practice and looking at price action you can do this yourself and do it consistently. Consistently — what do I mean? Every day Michael? No. One time a week finding something like this and working that position in like that. Absolutely."
