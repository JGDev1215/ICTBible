Opening Range Gaps (ORG)

30:25 – 55:21
30:35
How to See Opening Range Gaps
"You have to change your chart to regular trading hours — not electronic trading hours — and you'll see the separation between the settlement parts and the opening price on the new day."
→ Regular trading hours = visible gap between yesterday's close (5 PM) and today's open (6 PM)
30:33
How Long to Keep ORGs on Chart
"I'd like to keep opening range gaps on my chart for three days."
"The algorithm will refer back to those same levels — it's not just doing it today and yesterday — it's going to do this in perpetuity every day in the future. It's going to remember these levels up to three days. Sometimes it'll refer to it beyond three days but if you use three days as general rule of thumb it will serve you well. After three days take it off your chart."
→ Three-day rule: keep ORGs annotated for 3 days then remove
37:40
New Day Opening Gap (NDOG)
"The new day opening gap — NDOG — we have the closing price at five o'clock and then we have the opening price at six o'clock — so that separation is what we're showing here."
→ Wednesday–Thursday NDOG = closing price Thursday 5PM vs opening Thursday 6PM = the gap between those two candles
38:13
Consequent Encroachment vs Mean Threshold
"Consequent encroachment is half of any inefficiency or gap. Mean threshold is half of any order block. There's a distinction there — it's very important to keep that distinction and understand it."
Consequent Encroachment = midpoint of any gap / inefficiency / FVG
Mean Threshold = midpoint of an order block body only
33:52
Overlapping Opening Range Gaps
"After we get this opening range gap — the old one Tuesday Wednesday — we open above it, trade down and find support at the lows of it. But it didn't fill this — why? Because it has unfinished business — the buy stops up here."
→ Tuesday–Wednesday ORG and Wednesday–Thursday ORG can overlap
→ When they overlap, both sets of levels remain active for up to 3 days
→ This was described as "the first time I've ever taught this" (33:53)
50:22
Algorithm Refers Back to ORG Levels
"The algorithm will refer back to those same levels — it's not just doing it today and yesterday — it's going to do this in perpetuity every day in the future."
→ Use templates: one template holding all ORGs, cycle off after 3 days

Institutional Order Flow — Entry Drill & Sub-Minute Charts

24:52 – 25:28 · 57:53 – 65:23
25:22
Institutional Order Flow Entry Drill
"The algorithm will run down, create a fair value gap, trade up in here, breaks lower, fair value gap hits it — institutional order flow entry drill — which is a partial entry into a gap that does not completely fill — breaks one more time."
IOFED = price enters an FVG partially, does not fill it completely, then continues in the original direction
→ Not a reversal — it's a pause and re-entry signal
57:53
What Institutional Order Flow Is NOT
"This is institutional order flow — you're not going to see it on a volume profile analysis, you're not going to see it on a depth of market, you're not going to see it on a lighter, you're not going to see it on your level two data — all these things that you think makes you institutionally minded — it doesn't."
→ Volume profile, DOM, Level 2, depth of market = all can be spoofed. Price structure with FVGs and order blocks cannot.
58:26
What ICT Looks For Instead
"What I'm going to show you here is how it never surprises me — it never jumps off the chart and says I got you ICT, you didn't see that coming."
60:52
Sub-Minute Charts — ICT's Intraday Matrix
"In my charts, in my monitors, I have one monitor that I use and dub as an intraday matrix where I'm toggling through constantly — just by a couple keystrokes I'm going through five minute, four minute, three minute, two minute, one minute, 45 seconds, 30 second, 15 second, five second — I'm looking at charts just like that as I'm looking at key levels."
→ Sub-minute charts show FVGs and order blocks invisible on the 1-minute chart → explains ICT's precision entries
63:49
Why You May Not Need Sub-Minute

Balanced Price Range — When It Becomes Support/Resistance

65:29 – 68:12
65:33
When Inefficiency Is Truly Balanced
"Once we have this back and forth movement — that means it's efficiently been balanced. Once it leaves it — that means goes above it right there — it never needs to go back below this high."
→ Back and forth price delivery through the same range = balanced → departure = price should not return below that high
67:39
Balanced Range → Acts as Support or Resistance
"When they're balanced — that means up and down price delivery and then it leaves that range — then it becomes a real balanced price range and then you can trust that it won't go back through it. It'll act as either support if it's going higher or resistance if it's going lower."
Balanced + leaves higher = acts as support on any retest
Balanced + leaves lower = acts as resistance on any retest
68:14
Repriced ≠ Balanced
"Not just repriced to — when they're balanced that means up and down price delivery."
Repriced = price only traded back TO the gap (one direction)
Balanced = price traded back and forth THROUGH the gap in both directions

Three Drives Pattern & Mitigation Block

59:36 – 60:05 · 80:07
59:36
Mitigation Block — Definition
"It's also a mitigation block — which is this down closed candle — high, low, lower high. It's a reversal of sorts on my breaker pattern. So typically a breaker would be a high, low, higher high — a mitigation block is the down close candle in between the high and lower high."
Breaker = high → low → higher high (up close candle)
Mitigation Block = high → low → lower high (down close candle in middle)
80:07
Three Drives Pattern
"Boom, boom, boom — three times — this is the three drives pattern — very classic pattern."
→ Three consecutive tests of the same order block / level = three drives → then breaks lower (if bearish)
