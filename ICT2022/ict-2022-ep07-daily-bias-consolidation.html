01 — Core Concept

Daily Bias

"You're going to utilize the daily chart to determine whether or not the next candle is going to be likely bullish or bearish."

9:43

"In short, you're looking at your daily chart to get a read on where it's likely to go to next. Where's the price going to draw — is it going to draw higher to an old high, is it going to draw down to an old low, and until it gets that higher low you stay with that bias okay."

7:33

"If we had this setup on a daily chart — market structure has a shift lower, we trade up into a fair value gap — until we get down to here and here, each day I'm anticipating lower prices so my bias is bearish. Now every single day is going to be a down closed candle but I'm going to hunt intraday price action with that in mind."

3:43

"Between this high here and the midpoint of this low to high, I'm expecting down closed candles. That would equate to a bearish bias. It doesn't mean that I can't take a long entry. It just means that if I do take a long entry intraday I'm going to do it with far less leverage."

9:48

"Count how many up days or green candles there are from this high and this high down to that low... one, two. Real important. So the delivery by the algorithms are spooling — that means prices expanding and going directionally one way."

5:37

Daily bias: bearish run from high → low · ~2 green candles · consolidation at equilibrium = bias difficult

What Determines When the Bias Ends

"Until we get down to this low and underneath it, that means it's likely to reverse, retracement back in. So once it starts doing this and it's hanging around equilibrium it gets very difficult even for me to determine a bias."

10:21

02 — Leverage & Bias

Adjusting Leverage to Bias

Trading WITH bias "If I'm trading with the bias which would be bearish during the run from here to here and here, then I'm going to look to utilize the maximum leverage that I can have without overleveraging."
Max risk (comfortable) "I'll consider using my maximum risk which is 4 and a half percent. But I like to see trades that I feel comfortable with, with 3 and a half percent."
Trading AGAINST bias (long during bearish) "If I'm going to take a long, it would be counter-trend to that bias. My leverage is going to be dialed back."
For new students "You should be risking less than 1%, about a half percent or a quarter percent. Because if you don't know what you're doing there's no reason to be putting high levels of risk behind a move."

"You want to be indifferent to the results — that in the beginning is important. By having a demo account, using very very low leverage if not deleveraging okay."

5:06

03 — Consolidation Hurdles

What to Do When Bias Is Unclear

"When you have this condition it can make it very difficult to get a true reading on bias okay. So there's times when even me as ICT I don't have a good clear read on what it is I'm looking for. So I have to demand more price action and more information or Intel as I like to call it based on what the market's going to do right after the open at 9:30 in the morning New York time."

2:23

"No it just means that you have to rely on these smaller time frame intraday charts and just simply look for liquidity pools. So you're going to trade intraday volatility — running old highs, running out old lows — being a lot more nimble, and like a surgical strike, you take your handles or your points that you're trying to get out of the marketplace and then run. You don't try to overstay your welcome."

10:42

On Imperfection and Losing Trades During Bias

"When that occurs there's going to be times where you have an up closed candle that may be a day that you tried to go short and maybe you've lost okay, no big deal. Same thing going into the next day going in looking for shorts."

6:07

"You need to get over the concern about being right every single time. You need to put aside also the idea of perfection because you're not going to have it perfect. You're not going to get in perfectly, you're not going to get out perfectly."

6:40

"Being right is not equivalent to being profitable. Very important factor there."

7:26

"You have to have this embracing of the gray area in the marketplace. That gray area is where things aren't always so clear but because of experience you've had in the past seeing things unfold the way they hopefully would if you've been trained properly, then there's nothing to fear."

9:02

04 — 15-Minute Chart

8:30 Crosshairs & Relative Equal Highs

"So we have the 15-minute time frame and we have our 8:30 in the morning crosshairs here. Crosshairs are the vertical line that denotes the time that we start looking back to the left okay. Right here — go to the left."

11:16

"We don't want to use this low by itself because it's inside of this candle, so I want to use the extreme. Where's the liquidity going to be — is it going to be above this or is it going to be above that? It's going to be above this okay."

11:40

Relative Equal Highs — What Retail Traders See

"Keep looking left. What do you see over here? See that — these are relative equal highs. What does retail traders see that as? Resistance. So they see it going up to here, stopping and going lower, they see it going up to here failing to go up here. So now they think this is a really strong resistance level and it went lower. Look at that. Then the market showed how that level is real resistance by going right through it."

11:59

"This would be a first target here to reach for. And if you want to use a longer-term intraday target, would be back here — because to the left of this one is this one which essentially is the same."

"It's not enough to see how a low is taken out and then expected to go higher, or a high taken out and then expected to go low. There has to be some greater context behind it."

05 — 2-Minute Chart

Market Structure Shift — Bullish Example

"The market trades below the old low — trades below it, rallies above — but does it trade above this short-term high yet? No. Then it goes down once more below the old low, but not below this low here. Then it turns and goes higher."

16:19

"This low, its high, then we have one candle up, the next candle is low — there's your fair value gap. That's the run in here. So we have displacement low and displacement high. Is there a market structure shift, bullish? Yes. It trades up above it and you look for the fair value gap."

18:29

"We have an old low here. We trade down below it and at 10:04 I'm actually highlighting that candle right there. So that's where your stop would have to be, just below that, maybe one tick, by going long in here based on the logic I'm going to start to outline here."

15:55

Using S&P to Time the NASDAQ Entry

"We traded below the old low in the S&P, we took sell side out, we have a shift in market structure on the S&P... if we are expecting a fair value gap to be a launching point for the S&P and these markets generally move in tandem, I don't need the fair value gap in the NASDAQ because I'm utilizing the S&P as my indicator."

19:08 / 20:51

"I'm using the timing of that candle for my long entry on NASDAQ — trading what's not in the chart."

06 — Intermarket Relationships

Dow, NASDAQ & S&P Correlation

"These averages tend to move in tandem so that means they generally move in the same direction. So I'm looking at the Dow even though you don't see me trading it, I'm also looking at the S&P even though I'm favoring the NASDAQ."

19:18

"All of these markets move like boats in a high tide and low tide. All boats rise in high tide, all boats drop and lower with low tide. So I'm trying to get a read on this environment as a whole — not just my own asset class."

41:40

"What gave me the confidence that this was turning here? Well let's take a look at the three averages together. During that decline in the NASDAQ and the S&P, look what the Dow's doing — it's saying nah, I'm going to sit this one out boys. It's not willing to make that lower low where NASDAQ did, S&P did."

22:48

Dow holds · NASDAQ & S&P break lower · correlation cracks = "fingerprint of the algorithm"

"This is something that confirms an idea that you had already established before price does what it's doing real time. What does that mean? You're already looking for a reason to go higher. So that means you're looking at an old low — does it trade below the old low? Okay. Does it have a shift in market structure that's bullish? Yes."

23:32

"By itself it doesn't mean anything. So many students over the years have simply went in there and said oh this is magic, I'm going to just do that — and it didn't work out well okay. I blew accounts yes I'm not ashamed to say it, in the '90s I blew accounts doing this very thing — just looking for these divergences. And then I later learned that I have to have some kind of narrative as to why this would even form."

26:01

07 — The Macro

"This Is a Macro"

"The Dow is not going down because buyers are coming in — that's not what's happening here. That's what authors and book writers and people that don't really trade or code algorithms will have you believe. This is an unwillingness to deliver to that low."

28:01

"This is a macro. A macro is something inside of an algorithm that prevents or enables delivery — delivery of what? Price. So the Dow is unwilling to go lower here okay."

28:24

"Algorithms run on time and price — not price and time."

28:58

"Remember algorithms run on time and price, not price and time. The low — that's the price. But you have to refer to time first. What's the important factor of time? 8:30. Good. To the left — what's the first low? Right there, boom. So there's liquidity below that."

28:51

08 — Leaders

Probe Trades — Getting Intel

"On the floor, sometimes traders would have just a disconnect — they wouldn't have a read on what the price is doing. So what they would do — not just George but everybody on the floor would generally try to do this as well — they would simply go in and buy or sell short one contract and just see what happens."

32:19

"If you want to know how the market's likely to deliver, put some skin in the race. You'll have a greater feel for what it's doing. When you put an order in it doesn't need to be a large order."

32:44

"When I do that I'm trying to read what the price is doing against that order. I'm not trying to pick a spot when I'm tossing that in there either... I'm watching, I'm getting a feel for what the market's doing once that order's in there and I'm reading the reactions. I'm not looking at the equity okay... I'm watching how price delivers around that order. Is it easily moving away from that order, or is it lethargically moving away, or is it completely running against it?"

33:22

"That immediate feedback — that feedback loop that it gives you as a trader — because you have something in there that's worth something, you're going to be more attentive to what price is doing."

34:22

"If I'm trying to hunt the bigger move of the day I will sacrifice these initial little runs. I'm not doing it all the time."

35:07

"Sometimes these trades pan out and it's profitable — that's not the point. And sometimes it creates a small drawdown on the account. That small drawdown — if you count that as a skill thing, you're missing the point entirely."

36:09
