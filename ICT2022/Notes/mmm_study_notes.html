"I'm going to draw a distinction between the liquidity and the delivery."

Fair Value Gap — Definition & Classification

14:19 – 19:50
14:19
Two Reasons Markets Go Up
"Markets go up for two primary reasons: one it goes up to engage buy stops which is buy side liquidity, or it goes up to an inefficiency."
14:51
Fair Value Gap — Definition
"This separation between these three candles — one candle in the middle — this is what I call a fair value gap. It's a very specific price level that we look for."
→ Previous candle's low | gap candle | next candle's high = the 3-candle structure
15:10
SIBI Classification
"Because it's a down closed candle — what kind of inefficiency is it? A fair value gap classified as a SIBI — sell side imbalance buy side inefficiency."
→ Algorithm rallies back to reprice it; bodies tell the narrative, wicks are permissible
15:31
How the Algorithm Fills It
"The market rallies to these predetermined levels because it has to offer what — it needs to trade up back in between these two price points — this candle's low, this candle's high."
"The bodies tell us the narrative."
18:49
BISI Classification
"This right here is a buy side imbalance sell side efficiency — it's the opposite — an up-close candle. So it wouldn't be a SIBI, it would be a BISI — buy side imbalance sell side efficiency. Meaning the price will want to go back down to it eventually."
→ PD array classification = Fair Value Gap, but direction depends on candle direction

24:40

→ Overlapping SIBI + BISI in same price range
25:24

"These are trapped traders — this in here is a trap trader or trap traders that would have been caught short."
"In the classic days of commodity trading if you were to see old patterns like what would be considered an island reversal — this is the closest thing that I can kind of use to describe what's occurring when there is a balanced price range when it's extreme like this."
25:57
The Tell
"When we have a big decline, large range candle that's down, and then eventually — not months later, not weeks later — but when it's a big drop and a handful of candlesticks, not a whole lot of candles, then eventually rip back higher — that means that this was someone that's been caught short and they quickly repriced."
→ One big candle down + one big candle up, all price action below = trap trader → narrative goes higher
