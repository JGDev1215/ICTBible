Opening Prices, Power 3 & the Extremely Bearish Market

Risk on/off intermarket relationships, how to use the Midnight NY and 8:30 opening prices, Power 3 defined, and how to trade when the market is too heavy to give the ideal setup — on E-mini S&P.

Framework

Risk On / Risk Off — Dollar vs Other Markets

1:48

"Using the dollar index — is it useful? Like, when I look at the forex market, if the dollar is going higher, obviously that's going to put pressure on the foreign currencies and it'll be less likely for them to have sustained rallies, they're more likely to have sustained declines. So dollar up, foreign currency down. Dollar down, foreign currency up."

Risk Off

"Dollar if it is going up as we have here — this is a risk off scenario. Risk off scenario implies that generally every other market or asset class will start to decline. And this is treated as like a flight to quality, or a safe haven. So money pours into the dollar and it pours out of risk assets — that would be like foreign currency."

2:57

"This also applies as well to the stock market and index market."

How Intermarket Analysis Builds Confidence

"It gives you intermarket relationships and intermarket analysis to give you the trust… why did I hint that we're going to be going lower into the e-mini S&P relative equal lows and as we enter into May that's a seasonal tendency — so all those factors came together."

6:02

"Look at your chart folks — this is what it's like to be mentored. This is what it's like to know what's likely to occur with tools that make sense. There's nothing ambiguous here, there's nothing splattered across the chart — it's just simple sound logic."

Midnight New York Opening Price

"The opening price at midnight local time — New York midnight local time — that's what this here is. That's the opening price. This price is useful for trading the london session — that's two o'clock and five o'clock in the morning New York local time."

13:32

"It helps me frame the power 3 for the daily range. If I'm bearish I'm looking for something to rally above that opening price in London — that's my ideal scenario."

How to Read It (Bearish)

"If I'm bearish and it creates a rally above the opening price midnight time in New York, and it creates that rally — that little judas swing that I've explained many times already — and it starts to decline and I'm below that around seven o'clock in the morning, I know that I'm likely to create another little short term rally but it would be above ideally the opening price at 8:30."

15:12

"The beginning of the day — this is where I look for the daily range Power 3 setup and or the ideal scenario. From bear — I want to see something rally above that opening price. If I'm bullish I want to see it decline below that opening price. That's pretty much the basis for the opening price at midnight."

Concept

8:30 AM Opening Price — New York Session

16:07

"The opening price at 8:30 — I use that for Power 3 for the New York session. The same thought process I'm using for the opening price at midnight in New York time."

Definition — NY Session

"The opening price at 8:30 is the New York session's opening price. Now don't get confused with the New York midnight candle's opening price because the New York association in the name — New York session — is the 8:30 to 11 time period, New York local time."

16:21

"That opening price — for bears I want to see a move above. That's what I dub a judas swing, a false rally or a suspect rally. I usually like to fade those types of moves — as it goes up I sell right into it."

21:37

"Normally with the model I'm teaching you — to look for something to the left prior to 8:30, some kind of short term high, we want to see it rally up and using the opening price at 8:30 which is here, we want to see it rally above that, run some stops, then break down and create this type of scenario where it has displacement and an imbalance — it rallies back up into the imbalance and sells off."

When the Model Is Not Present

"The model doesn't exist in today's price action on the five-minute chart — it doesn't give it to you there." When the market is too heavy to rally above the 8:30 opening price: "You're going to have to leave out the element of a rally above the opening price at 8:30 and 9:30 just creates a lot of volatility."

Now it can be whipsaw where it can go up and down real short term and go both directions in a small range — sometimes it could be a really large range and clear both sides of the buying and sell — and then whatever the real move is going to be for the day, we wait for that to unfold."

Concept

Power 3

24:38

"I'm looking for this framework to offer Power 3 — which is a fractal element of accumulation, manipulation, and distribution. That's what Power 3 — the three things that is empowering you is understanding those three elements."

01
Accumulation

"Smart money would accumulate short positions."

02
Manipulation

"Anticipate manipulation above the opening price — they would assume more short positions there."

03
Distribution

"Then it starts to go the other direction and they distribute their short positions at some important low that we're able to forecast."

Power 3 Applied to Opening Prices (Bearish)

Looking for the framework at the 8:30 opening price for the New York session: "Accumulation — what's accumulating? Short positions. Why? Because it's bearish. So smart money would accumulate short positions. Then anticipate manipulation above the opening price — they would assume more short positions there. Then it starts to go the other direction and they distribute their short positions at some important low that we're able to forecast."

10:58

"Power three was in effect today. But sometimes it's really really small little movements like this and it may not be useful to you."

When the Market is Extremely Bearish

19:11

"Sometimes the markets are just simply too heavy and they're not going to rally for you to short into. It doesn't mean that they've changed anything. It doesn't mean that the concepts don't work. It just means that this is a market that is extremely bearish and when it's extremely bearish you have to trade it differently."

17:35

"We have extremely heavy bearish market — so it's not going to give us these stunning little short-term rallies that we can fade. It's just going to stay heavy and either you get in where you can find small little pockets of imbalance or you miss the move entirely."

28:01

"You have a little bit more insight as to what it's doing — it's too heavy, it's not likely to rally. There's no reason to look to go long. Start looking for reasons to get short and anytime you get an imbalance and we're above those relative equal lows on the daily chart — because it's going to draw down into that — that's the heaviness objective."

Why Prices Keep Going Lower (Draw on Liquidity)

28:23

"Why the prices keep going lower lower lower lower is because they're gravitating towards those relative equal lows on the daily chart. The algorithm is marching every minute, every hour, every day to that level."

Coaching: When the Model Doesn't Deliver the Ideal Setup

29:03

"You either have to have entries like this, which unfortunately isn't scalable with the model I've shared publicly, but I do have obviously opportunities to go over it like this and show you that how you can still use the idea of the fair value gap to get in with it — but you're going to have to leave out the element of a rally above the opening price at 8:30."

37:15

"It takes a little bit of courage to get in here and trade these types of setups. But that's what you do — or you don't do anything and you just tape read, you watch it."

Missing a Move

"You're going to miss moves, you're going to misinterpret it, you're going to read it wrong — that's going to happen. That's why you have to use a stop loss, that's why you have to be controlled about how many times you go into the marketplace and you also have to have some measure of flexibility and that comes with experience."

Concept

Two Fair Value Gaps — Entry vs Risk

20:57

"If you have two fair value gaps — you're using this as the entry and you're using this as the risk."

20:14

"This one here can be traded to on a spike — it can get up in there but your entry is on the lower fair value gap. Do you see the logic working here? You are going to use a stop that's going to allow for the market trading up into this one."

21:06

"Don't always assume that you can get up here and get the best entry because it never went there — see that."

Two FVG rule — lower FVG = entry, upper FVG = stop region (20:14–21:11)
37:29

"Just because it goes down lower it doesn't mean jam your stop loss to here because that's what happens. You need to have time and price behind you before you start moving your stop loss."
