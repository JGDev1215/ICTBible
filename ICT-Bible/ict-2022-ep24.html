Core Concept

Where Setups Form

32:22

"I've been teaching you to run above an old high or relative equal highs — and if you're bearish that would set up a scenario where the market goes up, sweeps the highs, breaks back down below the highs, short-term lows taken out with displacement, and then you go back through that displacement leg looking for a fair value gap to short into."

The Core Logic (Bearish)

"That's pretty simple logic isn't it — visually I want you to understand it. Can you draw out with a diagram — like if you were at work and you're trying to explain to your friend or co-worker what it is you're looking for in price — how would you draw it out? What would it look like?"

Bearish setup structure — relative equal highs → sweep → displacement → FVG short (32:22–34:37)
Bullish Version — Reverse the Logic

"This is going to be the sell version — okay just reverse it for the going long idea." Instead of relative equal highs, there is an old fair value gap above: "If you have an old fair value gap above you, and the market's below it and it's trading up — and you're bearish but you think it's going to go up to that fair value gap — the same logic: when it trades into that fair value gap, it doesn't have to completely close it. If it trades up into it, anywhere between here and here is good." Then wait for displacement below the low of the FVG and look for a FVG within that displacement leg to go long.

Concept

The Trigger — Short-Term Low Nearest the Sweep

The Trigger Defined

"As soon as it goes above [the relative equal highs] you start panning back through all this price running here and find the nearest short-term low — because that right there is going to be your trigger."

33:43

"It's not that it goes above this old relative equal high and then goes down below that — that's not it folks. That's not it. You have to see it go below that and displacement with the energetic move — take out a short-term low. That's how you filter out these trades that may not be high probability."

34:06

"That area right there — as soon as you run above the relative equal highs — in your mind you're looking for: where's the trigger? Because until it takes out that short-term low there's nothing going on. There's no trade there, there's no setup there."

Requirement

Displacement — What It Must Look Like

34:22

"Then when there's displacement — that displacement leg in price action — that right there is your foundation to the setup. That's it. Once you have that, then you can go into that area and look for the fair value gap. That's the logic, that's the pattern, that's what you're looking for."

22:50

"We should notice that we did have the initial criteria which is trading below an old low — so what's going to be resting below that? Sell stops. So they go down, run out the sell stops, traders are caught in. Then what? What's the next logic? We got to look for displacement — it's got to trade back above this old low, it's got to have displacement above that."

What Is NOT Displacement

On the day this episode covers, price traded below the old low but did NOT form the model: "It just went down, consolidated, returned back to it — but melted, didn't do anything above that old low. There was no displacement, no energetic move." Conclusion: "Anyone that would have looked at this and bought today is not using what I'm teaching — they're just impulsively pushing a button."

Core Teaching

The Two Entry Strategies

37:01

"That's the two entry strategies for this model. There's no other way of worrying about what does it look like. This is visually what you're doing."

Bearish Entry
  1. Bearish daily bias established
  2. Identify relative equal highs above current price
  3. Wait for price to sweep above those highs
  4. Find the nearest short-term low from that run — this is the trigger level
  5. Wait for displacement: "energetic move" below that short-term low
  6. Go back through the displacement leg — look for a fair value gap
  7. Price retraces into FVG → short entry
Bullish Entry (Reverse Logic)
  1. Bullish bias — expect price to draw up to an old FVG above
  2. Wait for price to trade into that FVG: "anywhere between here and here is good"
  3. Treat the low of the FVG like the relative equal highs: "same logic"
  4. Wait for price to take out that low with displacement
  5. Find the trigger: nearest short-term high in the run
  6. Go back through the displacement leg — look for a fair value gap
  7. Price retraces into FVG → long entry
36:28

"You can really make it simple and say okay — the low of the fair value gap, treat that like the same logic that you use for the relative equal highs. Doesn't that make it simple and easy? That way you don't have to worry about how hard do I need to know — you don't need to know that. Because you're waiting for something to happen. What is that? Once it takes out the low of the fair value gap and trades in there — you go back through that leg and see: here's that trigger, that's that shift in the market structure you're waiting for. Then you have the displacement leg. Once you have that you can go back in and look for the fair value gap."

Concept

Time as the First Element

22:05

"By itself it's just price — but we have to incorporate what element first. Algorithmically, it's time. So I'm teaching you elements of time at that 9:30 — we're expecting some volatility, dropping down into that area here."

Price + Time

The FVG identifies where the trade might form. But: "That fair value gap — that's the place in terms of price where should the pattern form. By itself it's just price but we have to incorporate what element first. Algorithmically, it's time." Both must align.

20:28

"I posted ahead of the 9:30 opening where equities open up and that volatility that comes around 9:30 — I want to see some kind of a manipulation in price. So if our focus is up there and it's dropping down and it's ahead of 9:30 — what are we likely to see? Drop down, get traders thinking it's going to go lower, and then rally up."
