Bullish Afternoon Pattern

"If you're bullish — generally what will happen is the market has ran higher in the morning, then it consolidates into the lunch hour, then after the lunch hour it'll drop down and sweep to sell stops or below some short-term low or the lows made in lunch."

20:43

"It creates a low ahead of the lunch hour at noon, then it drops down into the fair value gap — is that not doing what I gave you in terms of the rules of what liquidity is doing in the afternoon, post New York lunch when we're bullish?"

They think that is resistance, so anyone that's short they trail their stop-loss here. And drops one more time right below that short-term level. What's below that short-term low? I have already told you here — sell stops."

Bullish NY Lunch pattern: morning run → consolidation → lunch sweep → PM rally (20:19–24:15)
Key Distinction

Retracement vs Consolidation Into Lunch

The Distinction

"What's different? It's not consolidating — see that. Is this a consolidation? No — it's a retracement. So if you can identify an area where it's likely to retrace…"

21:33

"Because this is a retracement going into lunch — that to me it signals that they're going to work through lunch."

22:40

"Years ago when we were on the floor — only open outcry — sometimes the floor traders would know that the market was in a hurry. It's a fast market, so they're not going off the floor, they're going to stay busy on the floor and they're going to be trading. I learned this from an actual floor trader."

Two Scenarios

Consolidation into lunch: "I would look for the swing lows during the lunch hour and then wait for it to drop down, sweep them like it's doing here, and then rally."

Retracement into lunch: "Because this is a retracement going into lunch — that to me it signals that they're going to work through lunch." The algorithm is a fast market, already repricing without a consolidation pause.

Concept

Buying Sell Stops — Inside the Fair Value Gap

Buying Sell Stops Defined

"I want to buy sell stops because what that's doing — and how this fits into narrative — is if I'm thinking that this move here to here and then retracing down is setting up an afternoon run to potentially this level here by the close of the day, I want to be like smart money and do what? Buy. Sell stops — that is buying sell-side liquidity."

42:00

"Inside the fair value gap during a classic buy day — which is open at midnight, trade down, create the low of the day, rally — and then creates an optimal trade entry or fair value gap or you're going to buy the short-term low during SMT divergence."

45:51

"I want to do it because it really fortifies the setup — it allows me to get in where the smart money will be buying."

The Logic

When the market drops below a short-term low into a fair value gap: "The market drops down into the sell stops here and it's inside that fair value gap." This is the buy location: cell stops provide the liquidity that smart money uses to accumulate longs. The short-term low becomes the entry trigger.

Setup

Turtle Soup

42:57

"This is turtle soup — you have a low, lower low, buy the low that's being violated — with optimal trade entry."

Turtle Soup Structure

Three components stated explicitly: a prior low, a lower low that violates it, and entry using optimal trade entry (OTE — fib 62–79% retracement of the dealing range leg). Buy stop entry variant also described: "You can wait for it to trade down into this like that and then find the swing high prior to trading into the fair value gap and put a buy stop there as your entry — and whatever the lowest low is after it hits that, that's where your stop loss would be just right below that."

Turtle Soup — low, lower low (sell-stop run), buy at FVG/OTE with stop below lower low (42:57–43:53)
Three Entry Approaches at the Same Level

ICT outlines multiple valid entry methods at the same setup. First: "Low to high, pull your fib up — 62 to 79 level, if it goes down to that then you'll buy it." Second (Turtle Soup): "You have a low, lower low — buy the low that's being violated with OTE." Third: "Low, high — find the fair value gap below 50 level which is a discount, as soon as it trades at the top level of it, hit it there. Buy it there. Stop below the lower low."

39:43

"I'm adding the little compare tab — it's a little plus symbol up here if you go into your Trading View, click on it, it'll give you the drop-down menu for what instrument or market you want to compare it to. And you use NQM2022. This is going to change to NQU2022."

41:05

"Once it's down here you click on a little gear — and make sure it's highlighted low — because what we're doing is we're comparing and contrasting the lows."

When Bullish

"When we're bullish we're plotting this line down here as a compare and contrast tool. And it's SMT — smart money tool or smart money technique. It's my way of measuring real accumulation distribution."

41:51

"This low versus this low — you see that lower low. Is that seen here? Low, high, or low? We're comparing lows."

  • 01 Add compare chart: click the "+" symbol in TradingView, enter NQM2022 (or current contract symbol).
  • 02 Click the gear on the overlaid line and set it to plot on the Low: "Make sure it's highlighted low — because what we're doing is we're comparing and contrasting the lows."
  • 03 Bullish divergence: NQ makes a lower low while ES makes a higher low: "SMT divergence — lower low here, higher low here — we have the lower low in NASDAQ, higher low in S&P — that sets the stage for that run higher."

Practical Rule

Futures Contract Rollover

40:17

"We monitor barchart.com — and whenever the open interest is larger in the September contract than that of the June contract, that's when I roll over. So that's how I do it."

40:40

"These months or these contracts — they expire. June won't be traded anymore in a couple days and September will, and we'll trade September until a week or so into September and then we'll roll over to the December contract because September will expire."

Symbol Changes on Rollover

Current symbols (at time of episode): ESM2022 (S&P June) → ESU2022 (September). NQM2022 → NQU2022. Convention: "M" = June, "U" = September, "Z" = December.
