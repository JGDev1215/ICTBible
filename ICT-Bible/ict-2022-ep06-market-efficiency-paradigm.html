Bearish ICT Fair Value Gap

"The bearish ICT fair value gap — this is institutional order flow and it's a pattern that you can see the order flow actually coming into the marketplace."

10:20

Three Candle Formation — Precise Rules

Bearish FVG — Three Candle Formation
Candle 1 "The first candle is the high."
Candle 2 "The next candle is the extended low that goes below it."
Candle 3 "The third candle is another continuation candle."
Rule 1 "The first candle's low has to be traded below on the immediate following candle."
Rule 2 "The next candle has to trade with an extended low as well that went below candle number two but does not trade with a high that trades back to candle number one's low."
The Gap "That little space that's occupied right there — one candle only traded from the range of candle number one's low to candle number three's high. So that little space — that's the fair value gap."
Upper end "The upper end of the fair value gap is going to be the low of candle number one."
Lower end "The lower end of the fair value gap is going to be the high of candle number three."
Candle 2 role "The number two candle is always going to be where the gap resides."

Bearish ICT FVG: C1 low = upper boundary · C3 high = lower boundary · C2 = where gap resides

What Is Occurring in the Gap

"Between this candle's low and this candle's high we only have sell side offered. So that's like taking the paint and applying it to the wall and drawing down with the roller. But now as you pull down there's little pockets right in here between this candle's low and this candle's high where price has not been efficiently offered for buyers."

14:10

"To efficiently balance out that little inefficient area, at some future time the market's going to want to trade back into that area. When it does and you're bearish, that's a short signal — you can go short and sell there with the expectation it's going to start to move lower."

14:49

Optimal Condition for Bearish FVG

"Optimal formations of the bearish ICT fair value gap will be found after a run into buy side liquidity. So it's not a matter of going into charts and looking for this little gap all the time. This model I'm teaching you on this YouTube channel is meant for you to look for periods where price runs above an old high, then it breaks down, and then you look for this pattern."

15:10

"The run above a single high or multiple highs like a double top — either one of those fits this criteria. So what you're looking for is a pool of liquidity of buy stops resting above these highs — that's buy side liquidity. Smart money will want to trade up into that and go short."

15:45

"This is their saving grace right here — this pattern. That's what smart money is looking for. They're looking for that right there. And then once they see that they go in either with their limit order, market in, something to that effect, and get short. And the stop would be above the high."
