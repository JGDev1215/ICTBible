Daily Bias Framework, Weekly Expansion & the Economic Calendar

The simplified daily bias process — start from the weekly chart, use the economic calendar to find high-probability days, apply risk-on/risk-off intermarket context, and how to choose which pair to trade. USD/CAD and E-mini S&P.

Core Framework

Daily Bias — The Simplified Rules

Everyday Bias Is Unrealistic

"Everyday bias is unrealistic. If I go in looking for a specific predetermined daily bias before the market starts trading, invariably I'm going to have it incorrect."

20:24

"I'm not trying to get a daily bias every single day. I'm trying to determine the likely weekly expansion."

22:44

"In the beginning, all you're trying to do is determine: is it likely to expand higher? Is there going to be a big move, a flurry of activity going higher during the week? Or going lower during the week? And by itself — that is huge in terms of what you're trying to pick."

The Keys to Daily Bias

ICT states: "The keys to daily bias are simply this." What follows are the rules he outlines in the remainder of the lecture — weekly expansion direction + economic calendar timing.

26:34

"There is absolutely no expectation on my part to know the bias every single day of every single calendar trading day. I'm only focusing on these sweet spots — these low hanging fruit days where everything's coming together, all the stars are lining up."

Step 1

Determine the Likely Weekly Expansion

21:34

"I utilize a weekly chart and I study that — and I want to see: does it look like it's going to run to an old low? Does it look like it's going to run to an old high? Or is it running to an imbalance below the market price? Or is it trying to run to an imbalance above the market price? Or is it likely not to move because there's no data for it for that week?"

What You're Looking For on the Weekly

"I'm looking for the direction of an expansion move. I'm not trying to get the entire weekly range. I'm not trying to buy the weekly low and sell the weekly high."

23:41

"It's as simple as: is it aiming for and gravitating towards an old high to run above it? Or is it gravitating towards an old low to run below it? Or is there an imbalance where it needs to come back and revisit? I don't even need to know that — all I need to know is what direction is it most likely to have the majority of the volume pushing through."

24:24

"I want to find where is it likely to gun for this particular week — who's in the crosshairs? Have people been making money going long? Is there a low they're gonna try to run down and stop them out? That would be enough for me to frame an expansion going lower."

Top-Down Analysis Flow

"Identifying imbalances and price delivery top down — that means from the weekly down to the daily, down to the four hour, down to the one hour, to the 15 minute chart. And then once we get to the five minute chart you do the scalings from five, four, three, two and one — whichever has the clear obvious fair value gap for you based on the model's rules."

Step 2

Use the Economic Calendar to Find the Days

Which Events to Focus On

"I focus on days that have high or medium impact calendar events — only."

8:14

"Yellow events I'm not interested in. Red or medium impact — high or medium impact — those are the news drivers I'm looking for."

25:48

"If you have a news driver that's coming out on a particular day — say Tuesday or Wednesday — and you're expecting the weekly range to expand lower, well on those medium impact or high impact news events I'm going to see hopefully something that is going to run up higher, go into a fair value gap or run above a short term high, run stops, then break down, show displacement, then create a fair value gap and I can go in short on that."

1
Weekly chart: determine likely expansion direction — looking for old high/low runs or imbalances to reach for.
2
Economic calendar: find the high or medium impact news events for that week in the market you trade. "What day of the week and what time."
3
Kill zone + news alignment: "I'm doing that and looking for that setup and that directional price run inside my kill zones intraday at the same time the economic calendar is suggesting a high impact or medium impact news event is likely."
4
Wait for setup after news: "Really it's a matter of waiting for the news to hit the market and then create the setup. That's a little bit more conservative. There are times when it's so obvious that they're going to continue the move that starts ahead of the news driver — but it takes a little bit more experience."
29:00

"You ever hear the old cliche: plan your trade and trade your plan? Well this is how you can do that with that model. You look for the economic calendar, the time of day, what kill zone, in the market you're expecting an expansion high or low on that weekly chart. There it is."

Intermarket Context

Risk On / Risk Off — Applied to Bias

Risk Off

"Dollar higher and everything else going down." Foreign currencies, index futures, stocks decline.

Risk On

"Foreign currencies, index futures, stocks go higher. Dollar goes down."

41:14

"Dollar CAD — what is the currency's first pair in its name? Dollar. So if we're expecting it going down to a discount or running sell side liquidity, that means the bias is bearish. So if the bias is bearish on dollar CAD — that means it's a risk-on scenario. So the markets like S&P, Nasdaq, other foreign currencies are going to be easier to rally versus going down in dollar pairs."

44:41

"It's the fact that it's risk on, risk off — that's all that it is. I was focusing primarily on dollar CAD because dollar CAD had its high impact news drivers for today. So I was using that in relationship to what I would expect in the S&P market."

Using Dollar CAD to Frame S&P Bias

With a bearish Dollar CAD bias (lower dollar = risk on): "I found my trade by utilizing a market I'm not going to trade — but if it's going to give me insights and it's going to go lower, it helps me frame risk on, risk off."

Definition

Swing High — 3-Bar Pattern

Swing High Defined

"One candle, a higher candle high here on candle two, and candle three or day three — so this is what I dub a swing high." Three-bar pattern: lower candle to the left, highest candle in the middle, lower candle to the right.

5:00

"I'm not using a Williams fractal on MT4 — I don't use MT4. This is not MT4 platform. And five candles — if you're waiting for five candles you missed the boat."

5:27

"A three-bar pattern — one candle the lower candle to the left, a lower candle to the right — that's a swing high. Pretty simple stuff."

Time Concept

London Close Profit-Taking Hour — 10 AM to 11 AM

London Close Window

"That time frame 10 to 11 — that's London close. So if you're looking for directional plays and you're in a trade, you want to have about 80 percent of your trade off between 10 o'clock and 11 o'clock in the morning. Because usually — not all the time but usually — that's when it creates the opposing end of the daily range."

17:47

"So if it's creating the high of the session here, the low is likely to form between 10 o'clock and 11 o'clock — that's algorithmic."

18:00

"Now there are some times if there's news events — sometimes Canadian dollar's influenced by crude oil numbers — and that would sometimes skew this time window 10 to 11. So on those days when there's crude oil inventory numbers coming out and I'm interested in trading the Loonie, I will trade with the expectation between 10 o'clock to noon and not have such a tight little window of 10 to 11."

The Afternoon Continuation Window

On E-mini S&P — afternoon session targets: "That would be between 1:30 and 4 o'clock in the afternoon New York local time." This is where ICT expects afternoon continuation setups to deliver.

Concept — Refined

SMT Between Correlated Forex Pairs

45:27

"If you are trading forex — euro dollar versus cable, euro dollar would be a better trade than say pound dollar if there is an inability for one of them to go low — make a lower low — and one doesn't do that. That's showing you that's the relative strength leader — that would be the better buy in this situation."

45:50

"Another instance would be aussie dollar and kiwi dollar — New Zealand dollar. If you're looking at situations like this and you find a currency that fails to make a lower low intraday when it's risk on — you would be buying the risk on, higher low currency."

The Rule

Within a correlated pair (EUR/USD + GBP/USD, or AUD/USD + NZD/USD): if both go down but one fails to make a lower low, that one is the relative strength leader. "That's showing you that's the relative strength leader — that would be the better buy."

46:55

"Just because the news driver is in, for instance, the euro — the better trade may be in pound dollar. If they both went down prior to the news event coming out for euro, and if pound dollar failed to make a lower low — I'm buying pound dollar, because they're going to most likely move in sympathy with one another because they're closely correlated."

Decision Rule

Which Pair to Trade — Starting Point

How to Choose

"A lot of you ask all the time: how do you know which pair to trade? How do you know which one to pick? I'm starting that with the economic calendar."

46:22

"I'm not going to do anything except for focus on dollar CAD because the economic calendar suggested that's where the volatility would be for forex today."

46:40

"Sometimes I may use that market — for instance instead of being dollar CAD today, if euro dollar had a high impact news event I would compare pound dollar and euro dollar for correlation. So I would look for SMT divergences to signal which one I'd rather be in."

  • 01 Start with the economic calendar. Find the market with a high or medium impact event. That market gets primary focus.
  • 02 Correlated pair comparison. If the news is in euro dollar, compare to pound dollar. Look for SMT divergence — the pair that shows the higher low is the better trade.
  • 03 Use the driven market for intermarket bias. Even if you trade S&P, the dollar CAD news event gives you the risk-on/risk-off context for the morning session.
